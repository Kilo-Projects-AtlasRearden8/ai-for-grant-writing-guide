# 3. Drafting narratives with free AI

The narrative is the heart of most grant applications: it tells the funder who you are, what problem you're solving, what you'll do, and why it matters. AI is a drafting partner here, not the author. You supply the truth; AI supplies the words.

## What you'll need

- The funder's application guidelines (kept out of AI if they contain confidential info)
- Your program description
- Any data you already have (number served, outcomes, waitlist size)
- Your organization's mission statement

## The golden rule: AI drafts, you verify

AI can hallucinate statistics, invent programs, and sound confident while being wrong. Every fact, number, and claim in your narrative must come from you — and you must check it before submitting.

## Step-by-step: build the narrative section by section

Most narratives ask for some version of these sections:

1. Need statement (the problem)
2. Goals and objectives
3. Methods / program description
4. Evaluation (how you'll know it worked)
5. Sustainability (what happens after the grant)
6. Organization background

### Step 1: Turn your notes into a needs statement

Draft a few rough sentences about the problem, then ask AI to organize them:

```
Help me write a "statement of need" for a grant application.
Program: [one-sentence description]
Population served: [who, where]
Problem in plain language: [your rough notes]
Local data I can cite: [any numbers you have, or write "none yet"]

Write 2-3 short paragraphs in plain English. Do not invent statistics. Mark any place where I still need to find a number with [NEEDS DATA].
```

### Step 2: Write goals and objectives

Goals are broad; objectives are specific and measurable.

```
I need 3 goals and 3 measurable objectives for this grant:
Goal: [broad aim]
What success looks like: [your notes]
Time period: [e.g., 12 months]
Number of people served: [your number]

Write each objective so it is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART).
```

### Step 3: Describe your methods

```
Write a "program description" section explaining what the program will do, who will run it, how often it meets, and where it happens.
Program: [description]
Staff or volunteers: [who]
Schedule: [how often, how long]
Location: [where]

Keep it to 3-4 short paragraphs. Use plain language. Avoid jargon.
```

### Step 4: Evaluation and sustainability

```
For the same program, write two short sections:
1. Evaluation: how we will know the program worked (simple, low-cost ways to measure)
2. Sustainability: what happens to the program after this grant money ends

Keep each to one short paragraph.
```

### Step 5: Match the funder's questions

Before you paste anything into AI, read the funder's guidelines. If the funder asks five specific questions, answer those five questions — don't let AI write a generic essay.

Ask AI to check your draft:

```
Here is my draft narrative and the funder's list of questions.
Funder questions: [paste the questions]
Draft: [paste your draft]

Did I answer every question? List anything missing. Do not rewrite my draft; just tell me what's missing.
```

## Before you submit

- [ ] Every number came from your own records, not AI
- [ ] You answered all of the funder's specific questions
- [ ] Names of real people (clients, donors, board members) were removed or changed
- [ ] A person read the whole thing out loud to catch awkward phrasing
