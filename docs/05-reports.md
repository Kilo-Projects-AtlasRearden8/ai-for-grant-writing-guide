# 5. Writing reports with free AI

After the grant comes the report: progress reports, final reports, and outcome summaries. Funders want to know what you did, who you served, and what happened as a result. AI can help you organize your results into clear prose — as long as you supply the real results.

## What you'll need

- The funder's report form or questions
- Your records: number served, activities completed, outcomes, stories (with permission)
- Your original grant application (so you can compare plans to results)

## Step-by-step

### Step 1: List what actually happened

Before opening AI, write down:

- How many people you served, and who they are (in general terms — no names)
- What activities you ran and how often
- What you planned vs. what actually happened
- Any outcomes: test scores, attendance, feedback, stories you're allowed to share
- What went wrong, and what you'd change

### Step 2: Turn lists into report sections

Use one prompt per section. Example for an outcomes summary:

```
Write a short "outcomes" paragraph for a grant report.
Program: [one sentence]
People served: [number, described generally, no names]
What happened: [your rough notes]
Measurable results: [any numbers you have]
One story you can share (no real names): [optional]

Write 2-3 short paragraphs in plain, warm language. Do not invent results. Mark anything I still need to confirm with [NEEDS DATA].
```

Repeat for:
- Activities completed
- Challenges and lessons learned
- Next steps / sustainability

### Step 3: Check against the funder's questions

```
Here are my draft report answers and the funder's report questions.
Questions: [paste]
Drafts: [paste]

Did I answer every question? List anything missing or unclear. Do not rewrite; just flag gaps.
```

### Step 4: Human review

Read the report out loud. Make sure it sounds like your organization, not like a robot. Verify every number against your records.

## Before you submit

- [ ] Every number matches your records
- [ ] No client, donor, or staff names appear (unless you have written permission and the funder requires it)
- [ ] You answered all of the funder's questions
- [ ] Someone else read it before you submitted
