# 4. Budgets with free AI

Budgets scare a lot of people off, but they're really just a list of what you'll spend and why. AI can help you think of categories, write budget notes, and check your math — but the numbers must come from you.

## What you'll need

- A list of everything your program needs (staff time, supplies, space, training, etc.)
- Rough cost estimates for each item (get real quotes where you can)
- The funder's budget form or instructions, if they have one

## What AI can help with

- Suggesting budget categories you might have forgotten
- Writing plain-English budget notes (why each cost matters)
- Checking that your categories match the funder's terms
- Turning a messy list into a tidy table

## What AI cannot do

- Know your real costs
- Guarantee its math is correct
- See your funder's specific budget rules

## Step-by-step

### Step 1: List what you need

Write down every cost, even small ones:

- Personnel (who, how many hours, what rate)
- Supplies and materials
- Space or rent
- Training or certification
- Travel
- Evaluation or data tracking
- Indirect costs (overhead), if the funder allows them

### Step 2: Ask AI for a budget template

```
Create a simple grant budget table for [one-sentence program description].
Include these categories: personnel, supplies, space, training, travel, evaluation, indirect costs.
For each category, give 2-3 example line items and a one-sentence note explaining what goes in it.
Leave blank columns for "Amount requested" and "Other funding."
```

Copy the table into a spreadsheet or on paper. Fill in your real numbers.

### Step 3: Write budget notes

Funders often ask for a "budget narrative" explaining each line. AI can draft these from your notes:

```
Write a one-sentence budget justification for each line item:
- Program coordinator, 10 hours/week at $25/hour: [your note, e.g., runs the tutoring sessions]
- Workbooks, 30 students at $12 each: [your note]

Keep each justification to one sentence. Plain language only.
```

### Step 4: Check your math yourself

Add every column by hand or with a calculator or spreadsheet. Do not trust AI's arithmetic.

```
Sum check: does the total match the amount you're asking for?
Does each line item have a corresponding narrative explanation?
Are you asking for things the funder explicitly says they won't fund (check their guidelines)?
```

## Before you submit

- [ ] All numbers are yours or from real quotes
- [ ] You added the columns yourself (or used a spreadsheet formula)
- [ ] Budget matches the narrative (if you promise tutoring for 30 kids, the budget should cover workbooks for 30 kids)
- [ ] No donor names, grant amounts, or financial details about other funders appear anywhere

## Privacy reminder

Your own program budget is fine to work with. Donor data — other funders' grant amounts, donor giving histories, contact lists — is not. Keep it out of AI tools. See `docs/06-privacy-ethics.md`.
