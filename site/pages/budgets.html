Budgets scare a lot of people off, but they're really just a list of what you'll spend and why. AI can help you think of categories, write budget notes, and check your math — but the numbers must come from you.

What AI can help with

  • Suggesting budget categories you might have forgotten
  • Writing plain-English budget notes
  • Turning a messy list into a tidy table

What AI cannot do

  • Know your real costs
  • Guarantee its math is correct
  • See your funder's specific budget rules

Step 1: List what you need

Personnel (who, how many hours, what rate), supplies and materials, space or rent, training, travel, evaluation, indirect costs if the funder allows them.

Step 2: Ask AI for a budget template

Create a simple grant budget table for [one-sentence program description].
Include these categories: personnel, supplies, space, training, travel,
evaluation, indirect costs.
For each category, give 2-3 example line items and a one-sentence note.
Leave blank columns for "Amount requested" and "Other funding."

Copy the table into a spreadsheet or on paper. Fill in your real numbers.

Step 3: Write budget notes

Write a one-sentence budget justification for each line item:
- Program coordinator, 10 hours/week at $25/hour: [your note]
- Workbooks, 30 students at $12 each: [your note]

Keep each justification to one sentence. Plain language only.

Step 4: Check your math yourself

Add every column by hand or with a calculator or spreadsheet. Do not trust AI's arithmetic.

Before you submit

  • All numbers are yours or from real quotes
  • You added the columns yourself
  • Budget matches the narrative
  • No donor names, grant amounts, or financial details about other funders appear anywhere

Privacy reminder: Never paste donor data. See Privacy & ethics.
